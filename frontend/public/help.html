AjiraGuide · how to use it

AjiraGuide suggests technology careers from the skills you already have, explains each suggestion in plain language and shows the skills to build next. It works even if you can only list a few skills.

1. Create an account

  • Choose Create an account, pick a username and a password. Your full name and email are optional.
  • Or choose Sign in with Google: AjiraGuide receives only your name and email address.
  • You stay signed in on that browser until you sign out.

2. Add your skills

  1. Open Your skills and type a skill in your own words: a tool (Python, Excel), a topic (statistics) or something you do well (talking to customers).
  2. Suggestions appear as you type. Pick one, or keep your own wording: AjiraGuide matches by meaning, so coding and programming are understood alike.
  3. Add three or more skills for better results, then choose Save and see my careers.

If a skill had no close match, AjiraGuide says so and treats it with caution. Try rewording it, for example spreadsheets instead of sheets.

3. Tell us about you (optional)

Below your skills, About you asks for your education level, field of study, career interests and work or project experience. AjiraGuide compares what you write with each career's description and counts it towards your matches. Write a sentence or two in plain words, for example "I like finding patterns in data and explaining them to others." Leave it empty to be matched on skills alone.

4. Read your matches

Matches lists careers best first. Each card shows:

FitStrong, good or possible, from the match score.
ScoreFrom 0 to 1. It combines how many of your skills the career uses, how closely your profile matches the career's description, and how close the career is to your skills in the skills graph.
ReasonWhich of your skills the career uses, and how much it needs them.

Use the search box and domain filters to narrow the list.

5. A career's page

  • Why it matches: the plain-language explanation. Every statement in it comes from the score itself, so it never claims more than the system used.
  • Skills to build: what the career needs that you have not listed, most important first.
  • Technical details (collapsed): the score breakdown and a diagram of how your skills connect to the career.
  • Save keeps the career in your saved list.

6. Plan with the roadmap

Roadmap turns a career's missing skills into a checklist. Mark a skill as learning or done; adding a skill you have finished to your profile updates your matches.

7. Explore, saved and history

  • Explore: all 24 careers, including those your skills do not reach yet.
  • Saved: the careers you bookmarked.
  • History: a snapshot of your matches each time you change your skills or background, with each career marked as moved up, down or new.

8. Your account

In Settings you can change your name, email and password, switch between light and dark mode, and delete your account. Deleting removes your account, skills, saved careers, progress and history straight away.

9. If something goes wrong

"AjiraGuide is starting up"The server sleeps when nobody is using it. Wait up to a minute; the page carries on by itself.
"Can't reach AjiraGuide"Check your internet connection, then choose Try again.
"Your session has ended"Sign in again. This happens after signing out elsewhere or changing your password.
No Google buttonGoogle sign-in may be blocked by your browser's privacy settings; use a username and password instead.
Few or odd matchesAdd more skills, reword skills marked as loosely matched, or fill in About you.

AjiraGuide covers technology careers only, uses O*NET data from the United States labour market, and supports English only. Treat its suggestions as a starting point, not a replacement for a career counsellor.

10. For administrators

Administrators sign in at the API's /admin/ address. There they can manage users and their profiles, browse the careers and skills (read-only; they are rebuilt from O*NET and ESCO with the data pipeline) and open the System report: user numbers, cold-start profiles, popular careers and skills, and declared skills that matched nothing closely.
