# Study session script (about 40 minutes)

For the facilitator. Use only after ethics approval. Read the words in quotes as written. Do not explain or defend the tool during tasks. Note any point where the participant is stuck. Record answers under the participant's code only.

## 0. Setup

Assign a code [P__]. Open AjiraGuide on [device]. Prepare a blank row set in `study_results_template.csv`. Confirm the signed consent form is done and stored separately.

## 1. Welcome (5 min)

"Thank you for coming. We are testing a career guidance tool, not you. There are no right or wrong answers, and nothing you do affects your marks. You can stop at any time. I will not help during the tasks unless you are stuck, and I will write down where that happens, because it tells us what to fix."

## 2. Tasks

Task 1 (5 min). "Create an account with the study email I give you, then enter your own skills, in your own words."

Task 2 (8 min). "Look at the careers suggested. For each of your top three, rate how relevant it is to you from 1 to 5." Scale: 1 = not relevant at all, 2 = slightly relevant, 3 = somewhat relevant, 4 = quite relevant, 5 = very relevant. Ask: "Which of these would you consider, and why?" (note the answer).

Task 3 (8 min). "Open the explanation for your first career and read it as you normally would." Then ask the questions in section 3 and section 4.

Task 4 (4 min). "Open the skills gap for one career. Tell me what it says you still need."

Task 5 (3 min). "Browse the career list and open one career that interests you."

Task 6 (2 min). "Find your history of earlier recommendations."

Dead ends: if the participant cannot continue, record the step and what they tried, then help them move on.

## 3. Comprehension questions (per explanation read)

Write the answer key before the study, from the explanation actually shown. Ask three questions, in this form:

1. "According to this explanation, which of your skills matched this career?" (key: skills named in the explanation)
2. "What does the explanation say you are missing, or are not yet shown to have?" (key: gaps named in the explanation)
3. "Why was this career suggested to you, in your own words?" (key: the main matching reason stated)

Score each: correct, partly, incorrect. For analysis record correct (1) or not (0). Use the written key; do not accept guesses from general knowledge.

## 4. Uncertainty-note questions

- "Did you see any note about the tool being unsure about a skill or a match?" (yes / no; noticed)
- If yes: "What did it say, in your own words?" (understood: yes / partly / no, by the key)
- "What would you do differently, if anything, because of that note?"
- If they did not see it, show it afterwards and ask: "What do you think this means?"

## 5. System Usability Scale

"Please rate each statement from 1, strongly disagree, to 5, strongly agree."

1. I think that I would like to use this system frequently.
2. I found the system unnecessarily complex.
3. I thought the system was easy to use.
4. I think that I would need the support of a technical person to be able to use this system.
5. I found the various functions in this system were well integrated.
6. I thought there was too much inconsistency in this system.
7. I would imagine that most people would learn to use this system very quickly.
8. I found the system very cumbersome to use.
9. I felt very confident using the system.
10. I needed to learn a lot of things before I could get going with this system.

Scoring (for analysis): odd items score minus 1, even items score 5 minus the answer, add the ten and multiply by 2.5.

## 6. Job-readiness question

"Was there anything the tool said or showed that you took as a promise that you are ready for a job, or will get one? If so, what was it, and where?" Write the answer verbatim. Also ask: "How ready do you feel for these careers, and why?" (open; not scored).

## 7. Account and data

"If you like, I can show you how to delete your account and your data from the app now. It is your choice." Record whether they did.

## 8. Debrief

"Thank you. A reminder of what this tool is: it gives guidance based on the skills you entered. It is not a promise of a job, a placement or a result, and it can be wrong. Please treat the suggestions as a starting point and talk to a careers adviser before making decisions. Your answers are stored under a code, not your name. You can ask us to delete your data until [date]. If you have questions or concerns, contact [researcher] or [ethics committee]. Careers service: [contact]."
