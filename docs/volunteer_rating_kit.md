# Volunteer rating kit

The human part of the Explainability Quality Score (`docs/explainability_rubric.md`,
section 5). It checks what the automated scores cannot: whether people
understand the explanations, find them complete and readable, and trust the
recommendations more after reading them.

## What you need

- 5 to 10 volunteers, ideally students or recent graduates
- About 15 minutes each
- The rating sheet: `data/results/rating-sheet/rating_sheet.md` (ten explanations,
  generated by the current engine; regenerate with
  `python -m recommender.rating_kit sheet --run NAME` if the engine changes)
- A copy of `docs/volunteer_ratings_template.csv` to collect the answers

## Consent (read to each volunteer)

> This is part of a final-year project at Strathmore University. You will read
> ten short explanations of career recommendations and rate each one. It takes
> about 15 minutes. No personal information is collected: your answers are
> stored under a code such as R1. Taking part is voluntary and you can stop at
> any time.

## Procedure

1. Give each volunteer a code (R1, R2, ...). Do not record names.
2. Give them the rating sheet, on paper or as a shared document. Every
   volunteer rates the same ten items, in the same order.
3. For each item they rate H1 to H4 from 1 (strongly disagree) to 5 (strongly
   agree) and may write a comment for H5.
4. Do not explain the system or answer questions about an item while they
   rate; note any question they ask, as it shows what was unclear.
5. Enter the answers into the template, one row per volunteer and item:
   `R1,1,4,5,4,3,"what does proximity mean?"`

## Results

From `backend/`:

```bash
python -m recommender.rating_kit summarise --run human-ratings path/to/ratings.csv
```

This writes `data/results/human-ratings/rating_summary.csv`: for each question
(overall and per item) the number of raters, mean, standard deviation and the
share who agreed (4 or 5). Report the overall means of H1 (understanding), H2
(completeness), H3 (readability) and H4 (trust) next to the automated
fidelity, completeness and readability results, and summarise the H5
comments by theme.
